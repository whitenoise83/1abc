\section{Econometric Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{General forecasting framework}
\label{subsec:general_framework}

For target \(k\), forecast stage \(s\), and candidate model \(m\), define
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{y}_{k,t+h\mid t,s}^{(m)}
    =
    f_m\!\left(\It_{t,s};\widehat{\theta}_{m,t,s}\right).
    \label{eq:general_forecast}
\end{equation}
All estimation, transformation, combination, and model-selection inputs are
restricted to information admissible at the corresponding historical forecast
origin. The stage-dependent production forecast is
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{y}_{k,t+h\mid t,s}^{P}
    =
    \widehat{y}_{k,t+h\mid t,s}^{\left(m^*_{k,s}\right)},
    \label{eq:production_forecast}
\end{equation}
where \(m^*_{k,s}\) is frozen from the policy-development sample before the
confirmatory evaluation sample is opened.

The candidate universe differs by domain because the data frequency and
production architecture differ. GDP uses an AR(1), Bridge Ridge, a
Bridge--DFM ensemble, the Dynamic Factor Model (DFM), and a rolling
Bridge--DFM ensemble. Inflation uses a trailing 12-month mean, AR(1), Bridge
Ridge, and a Ridge--AR ensemble. Labour uses a trailing 12-month mean, AR(1),
Bridge Ridge, Factor Ridge, and an equal-weight ensemble. The exact frozen
stage mappings and target-level fixed comparators are reported in
\ref{app:policies}.

\subsection{Benchmark forecasts}
\label{subsec:benchmarks}

\subsubsection{Trailing historical mean}

For the monthly inflation and labour candidate sets, the trailing-mean
benchmark is the arithmetic mean of the most recent 12 admissible target
observations. It therefore adapts only through the expanding historical target
series and does not use contemporaneous predictors.

\subsubsection{AR(1)}

The common autoregressive benchmark is
\begin{equation}
    y_t = \alpha + \phi y_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t.
    \label{eq:ar1}
\end{equation}
For the monthly inflation and labour systems the AR(1) is estimated by OLS on
the admissible target history. The GDP implementation uses one autoregressive
lag with a constant and deterministic time trend. No lag order is selected
using the confirmatory evaluation sample.

\subsection{Bridge Ridge regression}
\label{subsec:bridge_ridge}

Let \(X_{k,t,s}\) contain the stage-specific bridge predictors available for
target \(k\) at forecast origin \((t,s)\). Ridge estimates solve
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\beta}^{\mathrm{ridge}}
    =
    \arg\min_{\beta}
    \left\{
        \sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{t,s}}
        \left(y_{k,\tau}-X_{k,\tau,s}'\beta\right)^2
        +
        \lambda\sum_{j=1}^{p}\beta_j^2
    \right\},
    \label{eq:ridge}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal{T}_{t,s}\) contains only historically admissible training
observations. Predictors are standardised using the training sample before the
ridge penalty is applied. The frozen penalty is \(\lambda=10\) for GDP and
labour Bridge Ridge and \(\lambda=8\) for inflation Bridge Ridge. Ragged-edge
and stage-specific predictor construction follows the data-building rules in
Section~\ref{sec:data}; no future observation is used to fill an unavailable
current-period value.

For inflation, the Ridge--AR ensemble is the equal-weight average of the
inflation Bridge Ridge and inflation AR(1) forecasts,
\[
    \widehat y^{RA}_{t,s}
    =
    0.5\,\widehat y^{Ridge}_{t,s}
    +
    0.5\,\widehat y^{AR(1)}_{t,s}.
\]

\subsection{Dynamic Factor Model}
\label{subsec:dfm}

The GDP mixed-frequency candidate uses a one-factor state-space representation
of the form
\begin{align}
    X_t &= \Lambda F_t + e_t, \\
    F_t &= A F_{t-1}+u_t,
    \label{eq:dfm}
\end{align}
estimated with the production mixed-frequency DFM implementation. The frozen
specification uses one latent factor with factor order one, AR(1)
idiosyncratic disturbances, and standardisation of the observed panel.
Estimation uses the EM algorithm with a maximum of 100 iterations and
convergence tolerance \(10^{-4}\). Missing monthly observations at the ragged
edge are preserved in the mixed-frequency state-space input rather than
backfilled with future releases. Further implementation details are collected
in \ref{app:econometrics}.

The static Bridge--DFM ensemble is
\[
    \widehat y^{BDFM}_{t,s}
    =
    0.5\,\widehat y^{Bridge}_{t,s}
    +
    0.5\,\widehat y^{DFM}_{t,s}.
\]

\subsection{Factor Ridge model}
\label{subsec:factor_ridge}

The labour Factor Ridge candidate first standardises the admissible labour
predictor panel, extracts four static principal components, and then regresses
the target on those factors using Ridge regression with penalty
\(\lambda=8\). Factor extraction and target regression are re-estimated using
only the information available at each forecast origin. This candidate is
distinct from the GDP DFM: it uses static PCA followed by a penalised target
regression rather than a mixed-frequency state-space model.

\subsection{Forecast combinations}
\label{subsec:combinations}

An equal-weight combination is
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{y}_{t+h\mid t}^{EW}
    =
    \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}
    \widehat{y}_{t+h\mid t}^{(m)}.
    \label{eq:equal_weight}
\end{equation}
The labour equal-weight ensemble averages the labour Bridge Ridge, AR(1), and
Factor Ridge forecasts with weights \(1/3\) each.

For the GDP rolling Bridge--DFM ensemble, raw inverse-RMSE weights are
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde w_{m,t}
    =
    \frac{\RMSE_{m,t}^{-1}}
    {\sum_{j=1}^{M}\RMSE_{j,t}^{-1}},
    \label{eq:inverse_rmse_weight}
\end{equation}
using only prior forecast errors whose target realizations were observable
before origin \(t\). The rolling history contains at most the most recent 12
eligible forecast errors and requires at least 8 observations. Component
weights are bounded between 0.10 and 0.70 and renormalised after bounding; when
the minimum eligible history is unavailable, the ensemble falls back to equal
weights. The resulting forecast is
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{y}_{t+h\mid t}^{Comb}
    =
    \sum_{m=1}^{M} w_{m,t}
    \widehat{y}_{t+h\mid t}^{(m)}.
    \label{eq:combined_forecast}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Stage-dependent forecast policy}
\label{subsec:stage_policy}

Define a forecast policy as the mapping
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{P}:(k,s)\mapsto m,
    \label{eq:policy_mapping}
\end{equation}
which assigns one candidate model to each target--stage pair. For every
target--stage cell, all candidate models are first restricted to their common
development-sample forecast origins. The stage winner minimises mean squared
error on that common sample; ties within \(10^{-12}\) are broken by mean
absolute error and then lexicographically by model name. This procedure yields
36 frozen target--stage assignments.

The fixed comparator for H2 is selected separately for each target. Candidate
losses are pooled across the common development samples for all stages of that
target, and the same MSE--MAE--lexical rule is applied. The resulting model is
then used at every stage of the target's confirmatory evaluation. The fixed
comparators are the DFM for GDP; Bridge Ridge for headline CPI, headline PCE,
core PCE, average hourly earnings, and payroll employment; Ridge--AR for core
CPI; and the equal-weight ensemble for unemployment.

\subsection{Stable policy and adaptive challenger}
\label{subsec:stable_adaptive}

The reported confirmatory forecasts use the frozen development-sample policy;
historical model choices are never revised using later evaluation outcomes.
This stable-policy design separates the question of whether a pre-specified
stage map adds value from the different question of whether an online adaptive
selector can learn from accumulating forecast errors. A strictly prior-only
adaptive challenger is therefore treated as future work rather than as part of
the H1--H4 confirmatory estimands. The post-confirmatory robustness exercises
vary predeclared fixed comparators, development windows, H3 stage aggregation,
interval-memory parameters, and multiplicity treatment without redefining the
frozen confirmatory policy.
