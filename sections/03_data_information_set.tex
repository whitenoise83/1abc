\section{Data and Real-Time Information Sets}
\label{sec:data}

This section defines the data objects available to the forecaster at each
historical forecast origin. The central organizing principle is
\emph{information set first, model second}: a forecasting model is evaluated
only after the data that would have been observable at the corresponding
historical date have been reconstructed. This distinction is essential in
real-time forecast evaluation because revised data and subsequently released
observations can otherwise enter historical model estimates inadvertently
\citep{croushorestark2001realtime,croushore2011frontiers,koenig2003useabuse}.

The empirical design distinguishes three objects throughout:
\begin{equation}
    \It^{RT}_{k,t,s},
    \qquad
    \It^{LV}_{k,t,s},
    \qquad
    y^{IR}_{k,t},
    \label{eq:three_data_objects}
\end{equation}
where $k$ indexes the forecasting domain or target, $t$ indexes the target
period, $s$ denotes the forecast stage, $RT$ denotes the historically
available real-time vintage, $LV$ denotes the frozen latest-vintage
counterfactual, and $IR$ denotes the initial-release target realization used
for forecast evaluation. The first two objects differ only in the numerical
vintage of observations that were historically admissible; the evaluation
target is held fixed.

\subsection{Real-time data architecture}
\label{subsec:realtime_architecture}

Let $o_{k,t,s}$ denote the calendar date of forecast stage $s$ for target $k$
and target period $t$. Let $x_{j,\tau}^{(v)}$ denote vintage $v$ of series $j$
for observation period $\tau$, and let $r_{j,\tau}^{(v)}$ denote the date on
which that value became available. The historically feasible information set
is
\begin{equation}
    \It^{RT}_{k,t,s}
    =
    \left\{
        x_{j,\tau}^{(v)} :
        r_{j,\tau}^{(v)} \leq o_{k,t,s}
    \right\}.
    \label{eq:information_set}
\end{equation}

The distinction between observation date, release date, vintage date, and
forecast cutoff is explicit. For each forecast origin, the real-time data
snapshot is obtained from the FRED/ALFRED real-time database using the
historical forecast date as both the beginning and end of the real-time
window. The observation end date is also restricted to the forecast cutoff.
Consequently, an observation dated after $o_{k,t,s}$ cannot enter the
historical information set, and a previously released observation enters with
the value reported at that historical date rather than its subsequently
revised value.

The information universe is governed separately for GDP, inflation, and
labour-market forecasting. ``Information universe'' does not imply that every
series enters every candidate forecasting equation. Rather, it defines the
set of admissible target and predictor series from which each model constructs
its own input matrix.

\subsection{Targets and governed predictor universes}
\label{subsec:gdp_data}

\subsubsection{GDP}

The GDP target is annualised quarter-on-quarter real GDP growth:
\begin{equation}
    g_t
    =
    400
    \left[
        \ln(GDPC1_t)-\ln(GDPC1_{t-1})
    \right].
    \label{eq:gdp_growth}
\end{equation}

The governed GDP information universe contains real GDP (\texttt{GDPC1}) and
eight monthly predictors: industrial production (\texttt{INDPRO}), nonfarm
payroll employment (\texttt{PAYEMS}), advance retail sales (\texttt{RSAFS}),
housing starts (\texttt{HOUST}), average weekly hours in manufacturing
(\texttt{AWHMAN}), the unemployment rate (\texttt{UNRATE}), the consumer
price index (\texttt{CPIAUCSL}), and the effective federal funds rate
(\texttt{FEDFUNDS}).

Monthly level variables specified as growth indicators are transformed as
\begin{equation}
    z_{j,t}
    =
    100
    \left[
        \ln(X_{j,t})-\ln(X_{j,t-1})
    \right],
    \label{eq:monthly_log_growth}
\end{equation}
while \texttt{UNRATE} and \texttt{FEDFUNDS} enter in first differences.
For the GDP Bridge specification, transformed monthly indicators are averaged
within the quarter. The mixed-frequency factor specification instead retains
the monthly indicators at their native monthly frequency and combines them
with quarterly GDP.

\subsubsection{Inflation}
\label{subsec:inflation_data}

For price index $P_t$, inflation is expressed as annualised month-on-month log
growth:
\begin{equation}
    \pi_t
    =
    1200
    \left[
        \ln(P_t)-\ln(P_{t-1})
    \right].
    \label{eq:inflation_growth}
\end{equation}

Four inflation targets are evaluated: headline CPI
(\texttt{CPIAUCSL}), core CPI (\texttt{CPILFESL}), headline PCE price index
(\texttt{PCEPI}), and core PCE price index (\texttt{PCEPILFE}). The governed
feature set consists of final-demand producer prices (\texttt{PPIFIS}),
average hourly earnings (\texttt{CES0500000003}), rent of primary residence
(\texttt{CUSR0000SEHA}), owners' equivalent rent
(\texttt{CUSR0000SEHC}), unemployment (\texttt{UNRATE}), the federal funds
rate (\texttt{FEDFUNDS}), the five-year breakeven inflation rate
(\texttt{T5YIE}), University of Michigan inflation expectations
(\texttt{MICH}), and West Texas Intermediate crude oil
(\texttt{DCOILWTICO}).

The price, wage, rent, and oil series designated as growth indicators are
transformed using $100\Delta\ln(X_t)$. Unemployment and the federal funds rate
are first-differenced, while \texttt{T5YIE} and \texttt{MICH} enter in
levels. The two daily series, \texttt{T5YIE} and \texttt{DCOILWTICO}, are
first aggregated to monthly means. The governed transformation is then
applied to the resulting monthly series.

\subsubsection{Labour market}
\label{subsec:labour_data}

Three labour-market targets are considered. Payroll employment is modelled as
the monthly change
\begin{equation}
    \Delta PAYEMS_t
    =
    PAYEMS_t-PAYEMS_{t-1},
    \label{eq:payems_change}
\end{equation}
the unemployment rate is modelled in levels, and average hourly earnings
growth is
\begin{equation}
    w_t
    =
    1200
    \left[
        \ln(CES0500000003_t)
        -
        \ln(CES0500000003_{t-1})
    \right].
    \label{eq:ahe_growth}
\end{equation}

The governed labour feature set contains initial unemployment claims
(\texttt{ICSA}) and continued claims (\texttt{CCSA}). Labour-demand
indicators include job openings (\texttt{JTSJOL}) and quits
(\texttt{JTSQUR}). Broader labour-market conditions are represented by the
labour-force participation rate (\texttt{CIVPART}), the employment--population
ratio (\texttt{EMRATIO}), temporary-help employment (\texttt{TEMPHELPS}),
the aggregate weekly-hours index (\texttt{AWHI}), manufacturing employment
(\texttt{MANEMP}), and industrial production (\texttt{INDPRO}). Other labour
targets may additionally enter a target-specific specification through lagged
cross-target information.

Weekly \texttt{ICSA} and \texttt{CCSA} observations are aggregated to monthly
means before transformation. Claims, JOLTS quantities, and industrial
production use $100\Delta\ln(X_t)$; \texttt{CIVPART}, \texttt{EMRATIO}, and
\texttt{AWHI} enter in levels; and \texttt{TEMPHELPS} and \texttt{MANEMP}
enter in first differences.

\subsection{Forecast stages and historical origins}
\label{subsec:forecast_stages}

Forecast stages are deterministic calendar rules except for the final
pre-release stage, which is anchored to the actual target-specific historical
release date. This produces a sequence of historical forecast origins
$o_{k,t,s}$ that is ordered by information arrival rather than by an arbitrary
fixed forecast horizon.

The GDP system contains five stages. For target quarter $t$, the
\emph{early-quarter} origin is the 15th day of the first month, the
\emph{after-month-1} origin is the 15th of the second month, the
\emph{after-month-2} origin is the 15th of the third month, the
\emph{quarter-end} origin is the last calendar day of the quarter, and the
\emph{pre-advance-release} origin is one calendar day before the actual
advance GDP release.

Inflation uses four chronological stages: month open, mid-month, month end,
and one day before the target-specific CPI or PCE release. Labour uses five:
month open, after week 1, after week 2, month end, and one day before the
Employment Situation release. Table~\ref{tab:forecast_stages} reports the
audited origin counts and median distance to the corresponding target release.

The stage-calendar audit verifies the exact calendar rule and stored
days-to-release value at every origin. GDP stages are strictly chronological.
Inflation stages are also strictly chronological wherever present. Labour
stages are non-decreasing in calendar time. In eight target months the
month-end and pre-employment-report stages coincide because the employment
report is released on the first day of the following month. Across the three
labour targets this generates 24 adjacent same-calendar-day transitions. The
stored real-time information-set hashes are identical for those transitions;
Section~\ref{sec:methodology} therefore treats them separately from genuine
information-arrival transitions when constructing the stage-information
contrast.

\subsection{Latest-vintage counterfactual}

The latest-vintage experiment is constructed so that it changes data vintage,
not information availability. Let
\begin{equation}
    A_{j,\tau}(o_{k,t,s})
    =
    \mathbf{1}
    \left\{
        (j,\tau)
        \in
        \It^{RT}_{k,t,s}
    \right\}
    \label{eq:availability_mask}
\end{equation}
denote the historical availability mask. A frozen latest-vintage extract is
formed by selecting, for each $(j,\tau)$ pair, the observation labelled
\texttt{latest} with the most recent retrieval timestamp. The counterfactual
value is then
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{x}^{LV}_{j,\tau,t,s}
    =
    \begin{cases}
        x^{LV}_{j,\tau},
        &
        \text{if a latest-vintage counterpart exists},
        \\[4pt]
        x^{RT}_{j,\tau,t,s},
        &
        \text{otherwise}.
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:masked_latest_value}
\end{equation}

The counterfactual information set is
\begin{equation}
    \It^{LV}_{k,t,s}
    =
    \left\{
        \widetilde{x}^{LV}_{j,\tau,t,s}
        :
        A_{j,\tau}(o_{k,t,s})=1
    \right\}.
    \label{eq:latest_information_set}
\end{equation}
Hence, by construction,
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{mask}
    \left(
        \It^{LV}_{k,t,s}
    \right)
    =
    \operatorname{mask}
    \left(
        \It^{RT}_{k,t,s}
    \right).
    \label{eq:mask_invariance}
\end{equation}

This restriction prevents the latest-vintage arm from acquiring observations
that are known today but had not yet been released at the historical forecast
origin. It also applies to lagged target histories: historically admissible
previous target observations are permitted to take their latest-vintage
values, while the current target-period observation remains unavailable
before its release.

Five daily \texttt{DCOILWTICO} observation dates present in historical
snapshots did not have a corresponding key in the frozen latest-vintage
extract. For these five keys the historical value is retained under
Equation~\eqref{eq:masked_latest_value}; no interpolation or neighbouring-date
substitution is introduced. This preserves exact mask equality. The exception
affects 12 unique-date information-set occurrences in the raw inflation
snapshot audit and does not leave any missing latest-vintage counterfactual
values.

\subsection{Ragged edges, aggregation, and structural missingness}
\label{subsec:ragged_edges}

An observation that had not yet been released is treated as structurally
unavailable information rather than as an ordinary missing value. Such cells
are not backfilled from future releases to make the real-time panel
rectangular.

The treatment of legitimate ragged edges is model-specific but fixed across
the real-time and latest-vintage arms. In the GDP Bridge dataset, transformed
monthly indicators are averaged within quarters. If a current-quarter feature
has no current quarterly value, its most recently known quarterly value is
carried forward and the affected feature is recorded. In contrast, the GDP
mixed-frequency factor dataset preserves missing monthly observations and
explicitly masks the target-quarter GDP realization.

For inflation, daily inputs are aggregated to monthly values before
transformation, and forecast features can use the latest admissible value at
or before the required lagged month subject to a maximum carry of two months.
For labour, the analogous maximum carry is four months, with weekly claims
first aggregated to monthly means. These rules are applied identically to the
$RT$ and $LV$ information sets.

Let $\mathcal{B}_{k,m}$ denote the production data-building operator associated
with target $k$ and model family $m$. The model inputs for the vintage
experiment are
\begin{equation}
    X^{RT}_{k,t,s,m}
    =
    \mathcal{B}_{k,m}
    \left(
        \It^{RT}_{k,t,s}
    \right),
    \qquad
    X^{LV}_{k,t,s,m}
    =
    \mathcal{B}_{k,m}
    \left(
        \It^{LV}_{k,t,s}
    \right).
    \label{eq:production_builders}
\end{equation}
Thus the same transformations, aggregation rules, lag construction,
complete-case restrictions, ragged-edge treatment, and feature-carry rules
operate on both vintage arms.

\subsection{Target realizations and evaluation samples}
\label{subsec:samples}

Forecasts are scored against the target as reported in its initial-release
snapshot rather than against a subsequently revised final value. Denote this
common realization by
\begin{equation}
    y^{eval}_{k,t}
    =
    y^{IR}_{k,t}.
    \label{eq:initial_release_actual}
\end{equation}

For transformed targets, $y^{IR}_{k,t}$ is computed from the complete
release-date snapshot using the same transformation as in
Table~\ref{tab:targets}. The scoring convention is therefore fixed across
forecast stages, candidate models, policy comparisons, and the real-time
versus latest-vintage experiment.

This separation is important for identification. The latest-vintage
counterfactual changes historical model inputs but does not replace the
evaluation outcome with a revised target. Consequently, differences between
real-time and latest-vintage forecast losses cannot arise mechanically from
using a different realization of the dependent variable.

The audited stage calendars contain 225 GDP target--stage origins, 1,202
inflation target--stage origins, and 1,739 labour target--stage origins.
Incomplete stage sets at the beginning of the inflation and labour samples
are retained rather than filled artificially; empirical comparisons use only
the forecast-origin pairs required by the corresponding estimand.

\subsection{Reproducibility and data-audit checks}

The data architecture is validated before forecast estimation. First, the
stage-calendar audit checks stage uniqueness, exact calendar rules,
days-to-release calculations, and chronological ordering. Second, the
governed series-registry audit verifies all 35 domain--series definitions,
including target/feature roles, frequency, transformations, aggregation
rules, and registry start dates.

Third, the vintage experiment is audited at both the raw-information and
model-input levels. The raw audit verifies exact historical-mask preservation
for GDP, inflation, and labour. The production builders are then applied
independently to the real-time and latest-vintage snapshots at every evaluated
origin. Across 3,391 model-input constructions---225 GDP Bridge, 225 GDP
mixed-frequency factor, 1,202 inflation, and 1,739 labour
constructions---both vintage arms build successfully and retain identical
indexes, feature structures, ragged-edge/imputation status, and latest
observable periods. Every evaluated construction nevertheless contains at
least one numerical real-time/latest-vintage difference.

The frozen latest-vintage extract, its retrieval metadata, and its SHA-256
checksum are stored with the replication artifacts. This prevents subsequent
revisions to the source database from silently changing the latest-vintage
counterfactual used in the empirical results.
