\title{Real-Time Macroeconomic Nowcasting with Stage-Dependent Model Policies:\\
Evidence from U.S. GDP, Inflation, and Labour Markets}

\author[inst1]{Cenk Ufuk Yildiran\corref{cor1}}
\ead{c.ufuk.yildiran@gmail.com}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author}

\address[inst1]{Independent Researcher}

\begin{abstract}
Macroeconomic nowcasts are formed under ragged-edge information sets that evolve
within the data-release cycle and are subsequently revised. This paper develops
a vintage-aware, stage-dependent pseudo-real-time framework for U.S. real GDP
growth, four inflation measures, payroll employment, unemployment, and wage
growth. The design separates the predictive value of incoming information from
the incremental value of stage-dependent model choice, compares historically
available with latest-vintage data under a common observation mask, and
calibrates 80\% predictive intervals using only forecast errors observable
before each forecast origin. Later information stages reduce mean squared loss
in 16 of 28 adjacent fixed-model comparisons, but only four improvements and
two deteriorations are significant at the 5\% level. The frozen stage policy
differs from its target-level fixed comparator in 11 of 36 target--stage cells,
lowers mean squared loss in seven, and produces four significant gains---all in
labour-market cells---with no significant loss. Vintage effects are strongly
target specific, with the clearest improvement for unemployment. Prior-only
empirical intervals deliver the strongest score gains for labour and core CPI,
whereas GDP intervals are conservative and sensitive to calibration-memory
length. Predeclared robustness checks preserve the qualitative conclusions,
although Holm adjustment substantially narrows cell-level significance. The
evidence favours explicit release-stage and vintage discipline rather than a
single forecasting or interval rule that dominates across targets.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data and Code Availability}
\label{sec:data_code}
Replication code, frozen design files, confirmatory and robustness outputs,
provenance manifests, forecast-origin definitions, and scripts used to construct
the reported tables and figures are archived in the public working-paper
repository at \url{https://github.com/whitenoise83/1abc}. Where raw-data
redistribution is restricted, the repository provides source identifiers and
reconstruction routines rather than redistributed source files. Commit
identifiers and artifact hashes used to freeze the confirmatory and robustness
analyses are documented in Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}.
